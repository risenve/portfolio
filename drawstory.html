Drawstory

Product & UI/UX design for an AI storyboard platform

AI-powered platform for instant storyboard generation, real-time collaboration, and client presentation.

I joined at the early MVP stage as Product Designer and took full ownership of the design: from competitor research and user interviews through to shipped features, a complete design system, and the product's web presence.

The problem

Traditional storyboarding is slow, manual, and siloed. Existing digital tools are either too complex for creative teams or too limited for production-grade work. There was no single environment where generation, editing, collaboration, and client presentation could happen together.

Research & Discovery +

I started by mapping out competing AI model approaches and running observational sessions to understand how creative teams actually work. Four AI generation models were evaluated side by side — each with different trade-offs in speed, control, and output quality.

AI model 1 — Script Upload: generate frames from a written screenplay

AI model 2 — Text-to-Image: direct prompt-to-frame generation

AI model 3 — SAM API: segment-aware image editing for precise frame control

AI model 4 — Object Generator: add or replace elements within existing frames

Initial user flow — mapping the end-to-end journey from brief to final delivery

I ran interviews and mapped user behavior across three key areas: how people gather references, how they work in teams, and where existing tools broke down. The patterns shaped the entire product roadmap for the next two quarters.

User interview notes — pain points and mental models from 8 creative professionals

Market analysis — competitor feature matrix and positioning gaps

Early prototype — first lo-fi flows tested with users before any visual design

Hypotheses & Design Process +

Every major feature started as a hypothesis — tested against real user sessions before being committed to the product. Here are the most significant design decisions and what drove them.

Reference collection — organising visual mood boards directly inside the project

Side panel layout — keeping tools accessible without crowding the canvas

Smart comments — threaded inline feedback, first iteration with auto-grouping

Comments final — simplified to frame-level anchoring; shipped without friction

Project type onboarding — film, animation, game, e-learning

List view — for detailed review and export

Grid view — default for internal team review

Animation preview — for client presentations

Impact

The product launched and gained real traction quickly — not through marketing, but through the clarity of the experience. When the interface gets out of the way, people focus on their work.

1000+ users on the platform
20K+ frames and scenes generated
50+ directors choose Drawstory for their projects

Beyond the numbers: Drawstory was presented at industry conferences and entered its first enterprise conversations — signals that the product had moved beyond early adopters and into a scalable market.

What I'd push further: deeper analytics on how users interact with AI-generated frames — which edits happen most, where people drop off, and what that means for the generation model. The design system is solid, but the feedback loop between design and AI behaviour is where the next layer of quality lives.
