Drawstory

Product & UI/UX design for an AI storyboard platform

AI-powered platform for instant storyboard generation, real-time collaboration, and client presentation.

I joined at the early MVP stage as Product Designer and took full ownership of the design: from competitor research and user interviews through to shipped features, a complete design system, and the product's web presence.

The problem

Traditional storyboarding is slow, manual, and siloed. Existing digital tools are either too complex for creative teams or too limited for production-grade work. There was no single environment where generation, editing, collaboration, and client presentation could happen together.

◐

Users strongly preferred dark interfaces — less cognitive load during long working sessions

○

Rounded UI elements tested as more approachable and trustworthy in user sessions

◎

Real-time team collaboration was a hard blocker — without it, adoption stalled

◷

Version history was expected as table stakes — users needed to revert without anxiety

◈

Inline comments and notifications were the most-requested collaboration feature

Research

I mapped the user journey from scratch, identified three competing AI model approaches, and ran interviews and observational sessions to understand how creative teams actually work. The findings above didn't just inform the UI — they defined the product priorities for the next two quarters.

Tab navigation for projects

Quick switching between open projects without losing context — modelled on the mental model users already had from browser tabs and code editors.

Commenting system

Contextual inline comments on specific frames, iterated from a smart threading model down to a clean, focused implementation that shipped without friction.

Scene Break with angle selection

A dedicated tool for splitting scenes with a camera-angle dropdown — giving directors precise control over AI generation without breaking the creative flow.

Project type onboarding

A structured selection page at the start of each project — film, animation, game, e-learning — so the AI model and default settings matched the user's actual context from the first frame.

Presentation modes

Three output formats — grid, list, and animation preview — designed for different audiences: grid for internal review, animation for client presentations.

Design system & UI Kit

A full component library built from the ground up — tokens, states, dark-mode defaults — to keep design and development in sync as the team scaled.

Impact

The product launched and gained real traction quickly — not through marketing, but through the clarity of the experience. When the interface gets out of the way, people focus on their work.

1000+ users on the platform
20K+ frames and scenes generated
50+ directors choose Drawstory for their projects

Beyond the numbers: Drawstory was presented at industry conferences and entered its first enterprise conversations — signals that the product had moved beyond early adopters and into a scalable market.

What I'd push further: deeper analytics on how users interact with AI-generated frames — which edits happen most, where people drop off, and what that means for the generation model. The design system is solid, but the feedback loop between design and AI behaviour is where the next layer of quality lives.
